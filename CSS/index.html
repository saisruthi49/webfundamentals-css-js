The Title

The old library at the end of the street had been closed for years, but every evening a soft yellow light still glowed behind its dusty windows. People in the neighbourhood said it was only the setting sun reflecting off the glass, yet those who walked past slowly enough could hear the faint rustle of pages turning. Inside, rows of tall wooden shelves leaned against each other like tired friends, holding thousands of books that nobody had borrowed in a long time. A thin layer of dust covered the reading tables, and a single clock above the door had stopped at ten minutes past four. Children who dared to peek through the keyhole claimed they saw a gentle shadow moving between the aisles, carefully placing each book back where it belonged. One rainy afternoon, a young girl named Meera finally gathered the courage to push open the heavy front door. It creaked loudly, and for a moment the whole building seemed to hold its breath. She stepped inside, her shoes echoing on the stone floor, and noticed a small brass lamp glowing on the librarian's desk. Beside it lay an open notebook with a pen resting on the page, as if someone had just stepped away. Meera smiled, picked up the pen, and began to write her own name at the bottom of the list of readers, and somewhere deep within the shelves, the pages began to turn again.
